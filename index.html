How to read the rows

  • Tap a row to keep your place. A second tap undoes it. Nothing is recorded.
  • StopThe one or two things on a procedure that are most dangerous to skip. Answer each aloud before you start.
  • EmergencyA red-bordered card at the top of intubation, central line and chest tube. It says when to stop working the list and act.
  • 3Three pauses before the procedure, none longer than seven items, then one huddle after. At a pause everyone in the room stops and everyone speaks.

What this is

A script for the conversation between a fellow and an attending before an invasive procedure on a child. The supervision policy for each procedure is shown first, in its own words. The checklist follows it.

Tick boxes to keep your place; the ticks are gone when the page reloads. Nothing here accepts typing, so no patient identifier can be entered.

How to use it

  • Open the procedure. Read the supervision policy. Decide, with the attending, who will be where.
  • Work the three pauses in order. The fellow reads; each person answers for their own part.
  • Tap Note under a row for the reason, the source, or a number you will need.
  • After the procedure, and before anyone leaves, run the two-minute huddle. Then the feedback form.
  • Reset is top-right and asks twice. Pull-to-refresh is blocked.

Why three pauses and a huddle

The one pre-procedure bundle with multicentre pediatric evidence of sustained harm reduction has four parts: risk-factor assessment, plan generation, a pre-procedure time-out, and a post-procedure huddle (Li 2014, Am J Med Qual, PMID 25143411). Across 15 PICUs it cut adverse tracheal-intubation-associated events from 17.5% to 13.7%, adjusted OR 0.63 (95% CI 0.47–0.83), and the effect held at 24 months (Nishisaki 2021, Crit Care Med, PMID 33177363). The institutional document carries the first two parts; this tool carries all four. No pause here runs past seven items.

Where the lines come from

All 88 lines of the division’s Pre-Procedure Checklists document are here, wording unchanged. Everything else came from published evidence and carries no institutional authority — the three pause points and the huddle, the time-out and attempt-limit rows, the laterality, sterile-barrier, coagulation and confirmation rows, the emergency bypass cards, and every reference table inside a Note. These are no longer marked row by row.

The source document has no supervision-plan line on intubation, chest tube or sedation, never names a side for the chest tube, and has no infection-prevention line for the central line. A row was added for each. Two numbers are still missing on purpose. No age-banded hypotension cut-point and no ETT depth-at-the-lip formula appear anywhere on this page, because no source for either was resolved.

Screen and print

The page follows the phone’s light or dark setting. Printing renders dark on light with every note expanded, for the laminated copy on the line cart.

Supervision policy, read before the checklist

All central venous lines placed by a first-year fellow in the first 6 months of the academic year should be directly supervised by a faculty member (+/- supervisory fellow). When a procedure is being completed by a first-year fellow with the supervision of a senior fellow, faculty are expected to supervise in a way that feedback can be provided to both the first-year fellow and supervising fellow. All central venous lines placed by a senior fellow should be placed with faculty support immediately available.

Faculty bedside presence is required for all natural airway sedations.

  3. Note

Pause 2

Before you draw up

  3. Note
    Add immobility as a fourth. It usually sets your depth for a line or a lumbar puncture in a toddler, and depth drives airway risk.
  4. Note
    Ketamine, propofol, dexmedetomidine, lorazepam. No doses are printed here; use the unit’s weight-based reference. Weight in kg from the chart, and every dose read back by a second person. Local anesthetic maximum calculated and checked against the syringe volume before the field is prepped.
  5. Note
    Open and tested before the first dose. Suction connected and running, a self-inflating bag with a mask that fits this face, oxygen flowing, an oral airway and a supraglottic airway sized to this child, a laryngoscope and tubes, and vascular access you have already flushed. The person watching the child is the rescuer, and the rescue equipment is theirs to check.Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
  6. Note
    Ask the airway history first — prior difficult intubation or mask ventilation, tracheostomy or subglottic stenosis, a syndrome, a recent extubation failure. Suction connected, on, tested, catheter within reach. Oral and nasopharyngeal airways out, with a size either side of your first choice. A supraglottic airway sized by weight; the LMA Classic bands are in the Airway Adjuncts note on the intubation checklist.Difficult bag-mask ventilation, 9.5% of 15,810 PICU intubations, adverse-event odds ratio 2.28 (2.03–2.57). Daigle 2020, PMID 32590390.
  7. Note
    Reversal agents for what you are giving, plus epinephrine and atropine drawn or immediately available, dosed to this child’s weight in kg.Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
