How to read the rows

  • Solid box, no tag — an item from the WashU PICU Pre-Procedure Checklists document, wording unchanged. All 88 are here.
  • Added Dashed box — an addition this tool offers from published evidence. It is not institutional policy. Its reason is in the Note beneath it.
  • StopThe one or two things on a procedure that are most dangerous to skip. Do not start until each has been answered aloud.
  • EmergencyA red-bordered card, not a tickable row. It tells you when to stop working the list and act. It sits at the top of intubation, central line and chest tube.
  • 3Three pauses before the procedure, no pause longer than seven items, then one huddle after. A pause point is a stop: everyone in the room, everyone speaks.

What this is

A script for the conversation between a fellow and an attending before an invasive procedure on a child. The supervision policy for each procedure is shown first, in its own words, because it is the reason the document exists. The checklist follows it.

It is not a form and it is not an assessment. Tick boxes to keep your place; the ticks are gone when the page reloads. Nothing on this page accepts typing, so no patient identifier can be entered.

How to use it

  • Open the procedure. Read the supervision policy. Decide, with the attending, who will be where.
  • Work the three pauses in order. Each pause is a stop at a named moment; the fellow reads, and each person in the room answers for their own part. Second tap on a row undoes it.
  • Rows marked Added are offered, not mandated. Tap Note under a row for the reason and the source. Notes carry the reason, the source, and sometimes a number you will need. Open the note on any row you are unsure about.
  • After the procedure, before anyone leaves: the two-minute huddle. Then, and only then, the feedback form.
  • Reset is top-right and asks twice. Pull-to-refresh is blocked so a stray swipe does not clear the list.

Why three pauses and a huddle

The one pre-procedure bundle with multicentre pediatric evidence of sustained harm reduction has four parts: risk-factor assessment, plan generation, a pre-procedure time-out, and a post-procedure huddle (Li 2014, Am J Med Qual, PMID 25143411). Across 15 PICUs it cut adverse tracheal-intubation-associated events from 17.5% to 13.7%, adjusted OR 0.63 (95% CI 0.47–0.83), and the effect held at 24 months (Nishisaki 2021, Crit Care Med, PMID 33177363). The institutional document carries the first two parts. This tool adds the last two, marked as additions.

The ceiling that matters for a checklist is items per pause point rather than items per checklist; the WHO surgical checklist runs 19 items as 7 / 7 / 5. No pause here runs past seven, so a fellow sees a screenful at a time and never the whole list at once. Round 2 cut the count further: additions that repeated an item already in the time-out were folded into it, and arterial line and lumbar puncture each lost five boxes.

Notes on the source document

These are surfaced rather than silently fixed. Every source line ships unchanged.

  • The Procedural Supervision Plan line appears on Central Venous Line, Arterial Line and Lumbar Puncture but not on Intubation, Chest Tube or Natural Airway Sedation. The policy expects faculty supervision for all intubations, direct bedside supervision for chest tubes, and faculty presence for all sedations. The policy text is shown on all six, and a supervision-plan row is added to chest tube and to sedation.
  • The Chest Tube section never names a side. A side-confirmation row is added and marked Stop.
  • The Central Venous Line section has no infection-prevention item. A sterile-barrier row is added.
  • The escalation trigger is stated as a maximum attempt count, agreed aloud, rather than left to judgment mid-procedure. In children with known difficult airways, complications were associated with more than two intubation attempts (Fiadjoe 2016, Lancet Respir Med, PMID 26705976). No pediatric attempt threshold was found for vascular access or lumbar puncture, so those rows ask the team to state a number.
  • Age-banded hypotension cut-points are still left out, because no source was resolved, and so is any ETT depth-at-the-lip formula. Tube size is now on the ETT row, taken from the manufacturer’s own age chart rather than from a formula. A laminated card with a wrong pediatric number is worse than one with none.

Screen and print

The page follows the phone’s light or dark setting. In a dim room at night the dark theme keeps the screen from lighting the bed space, and every contrast ratio is held in both themes. Printing always renders dark on light with every note expanded, for the laminated copy on the line cart.

    Induction agentDoseRocuronium0.6 mg/kg IVEtomidate0.3 mg/kg IV over 30–60 sFentanyl2–3 mcg/kg IVKetamine2 mg/kg IVAtropine, for bradycardia0.02 mg/kg IV/IO

    Rocuronium. 0.45 mg/kg is the label’s alternative. The length of the block is the problem rather than the pressure: at 0.6 mg/kg the label reports 36.7 to 59.8 minutes to reappearance of T3 from neonates through children, so nobody is rescued by spontaneous recovery.

    Etomidate. A single 0.3 mg/kg dose lowers plasma cortisol for six to eight hours, and transient skeletal muscle movements were seen in about 32%. The label’s range is 0.2 to 0.6 mg/kg, and it does not establish hemodynamic neutrality in a shocked child.

    Fentanyl. 2 to 3 mcg/kg is the label’s figure for induction and maintenance in children 2 to 12 years. Rigidity of the muscles of respiration is dose-related and is what turns a rigidity event into an airway event; bradycardia is also listed.

    Ketamine. 2 mg/kg is the label’s average for 5 to 10 minutes of surgical anesthesia within 30 seconds, inside a full IV range of 1 to 4.5 mg/kg. It raises blood pressure, heart rate and cardiac output, although in suspected catecholamine depletion the label names cardiac decompensation.

    No shock-adjusted number is printed here, because none could be sourced. Bring the dose down and watch the pressure; the figure is a bedside judgment, not a card.

    Rocuronium bromide injection (Caplin Steriles, rev. May 2024), AMIDATE etomidate (Hospira, rev. 04/2022), SUBLIMAZE fentanyl citrate (Taylor Pharmaceuticals, rev. 2008) and KETALAR ketamine (Par Health USA, rev. 2026), all read on DailyMed this session. Three carry a pediatric limitation worth knowing: rocuronium’s label does not recommend it for pediatric rapid sequence intubation, etomidate’s does not recommend use below age 10, and ketamine’s does not establish safety below 16, so PICU practice with all three is partly off-label. Atropine 0.02 mg/kg with no minimum and a 0.5 mg ceiling is the unit’s figure. The FDA label (Atropine Sulfate Injection, Hospira, NDA 021146, rev. 11/2020, read this session) states that pediatric dosing has not been well studied, gives a usual initial dose of 0.01 to 0.03 mg/kg, and sets no pediatric minimum and no pediatric maximum. Difficult mask ventilation was reported more often when neuromuscular blockade was not used. Daigle 2020, PMID 32590390.

    For the internal jugular, Trendelenburg with a shoulder roll to extend the neck is common to both sides of the argument, although how far to turn the head is genuinely contested. Rotation makes the vein bigger and slides it over the carotid at the same time, and the two pediatric ultrasound studies reach opposite recommendations. Scan the neck in the position you intend to puncture in, rather than assuming a default angle is safe.

    This is internal-jugular guidance. Nothing site-specific for the femoral or subclavian route was sourced, so nothing is offered for them here.

Arai 2009 (Paediatr Anaesth), PMID 19691692. Both agree rotation increases overlap; they disagree on whether the larger vein is worth it.

  3. Note
    French size and the depth to which you intend to insert. Children have thoracic anatomy and physiology that differ from adults, and that changes sizing.

    Pigtail catheters. In the pediatric trauma literature a pigtail means a percutaneously placed catheter of 14 Fr or smaller. In the largest pediatric series the pigtails ran to a median of 14 Fr (IQR 12–14) and the conventional tubes to a median of 28 Fr (IQR 24–32), although that cohort’s median age was about 15.7 years, so it says nothing about a toddler.

    Seldinger kits. The Thal-Quick set is made in 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 28 and 32 Fr. The three smallest are short, at 18, 20 and 22 cm; everything from 14 Fr upward is 41 cm. Those are the sizes manufactured rather than a recommendation of which to use in whom, and the instructions for use carry no pediatric size selection at all.

    No French-by-weight table appears on this page, for pigtails or for conventional tubes, because none could be sourced. Size to the child and the indication in front of you, and say the number aloud.

    A pigtail is not the answer to a tension pneumothorax. The pediatric evidence excluded unstable children by design.

    548 hemodynamically stable pediatric trauma patients with hemothorax or hemopneumothorax at 41 centres, 477 chest tubes against 71 pigtails: failure 17.6% against 12.6% (p=0.38), adjusted odds ratio for failure 0.95 (0.87–1.04); complications 11.9% against 19.7% unadjusted (p=0.02) but 1.05 (0.95–1.15) adjusted, driven by ventilator-associated pneumonia; ten empyemas, all of them in the chest-tube group. Level of evidence IV. The authors excluded unstable patients because a pigtail may not be appropriate for a child who needs an emergent finger thoracostomy, and they say the difference in the youngest children remains to be worked out. Natural Airway Sedation

Supervision policy, read before the checklist

Faculty bedside presence is required for all natural airway sedations.

Quoted verbatim from the division’s procedural supervision policy. Nothing on this page is computed from fellow year; the policy’s own words decide.

Start the checklist
Pause 1

Before you commit

Fellow and attending. The attending is at the bedside for this one.

  3. Note
    Prior difficult mask or intubation; syndromic or craniofacial features; large (kissing) tonsils; obstructive symptoms; limited neck movement; weight under 10 kg. If any, the sedating clinician is the rescuer: plan the rescue before the first dose.A focused airway examination for large (kissing) tonsils or anatomic airway abnormality is a stated requirement of the AAP/AAPD sedation guideline. Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666. Difficult bag-mask ventilation: 9.5% of PICU intubations, adverse-event OR 2.28. Daigle 2020, PMID 32590390.
  4. Note
    Hours per unit policy, not printed here. The guideline asks for fasting before elective procedures and for a balance between depth of sedation and risk when the child cannot fast because the procedure is urgent.Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
  5. Note
    A remote location changes what is within reach, not what is required. In an MRI suite that means MRI-conditional monitoring and an infusion setup, a rescue kit and airway equipment staged where you can reach them, an unobstructed line of sight to the monitor, and an agreed way to get the child out of the bore.The room-specific detail is offered as practice. The guideline requires age- and size-appropriate airway and venous-access equipment, appropriate physiologic monitoring during and after the procedure, and enough trained staff to both do the procedure and monitor the patient. Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
Pause 2

Before you draw up

Fellow with the bedside nurse. Open it and test it now; there is no time to look for it later.

  3. Note
    Add immobility as a fourth. It is usually the goal that sets your depth for a line or a lumbar puncture in a toddler, and depth is what drives airway risk.
  4. Note
    Weight in kg from the chart, not estimated. Every dose mg/kg, read back by a second person. Local anesthetic maximum calculated and checked against the syringe volume before the field is prepped.

    Ketamine, IV. The label’s average for 5 to 10 minutes of surgical anesthesia within 30 seconds is 2 mg/kg, inside a full IV range of 1 to 4.5 mg/kg. It does not suppress pharyngeal and laryngeal reflexes, which is not the same as a guaranteed patent airway.

    Dexmedetomidine, MRI, as a sole agent. 2 mcg/kg IV over ten minutes, then 1 mcg/kg/hr. It held alone in 78.5% of 544 children; bradycardia 3.9% and hypotension 18.4%, none of which needed intervention.

    A pediatric procedural-sedation dose from a society guideline could not be opened this session, so none is printed. Titrate against the unit’s own reference.

    Two agents do not belong on a native airway. A neuromuscular blocker never does: rocuronium’s label reports 36.7 to 59.8 minutes to reappearance of T3 in children at 0.6 mg/kg, and no sedation plan survives that. Etomidate is an induction agent, and its label does not recommend it below age 10.

    Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666. KETALAR ketamine (Par Health USA), rocuronium bromide injection (Caplin Steriles) and AMIDATE etomidate (Hospira) labels, all read on DailyMed this session; ketamine’s label does not establish safety below age 16. Dexmedetomidine: a retrospective single-institution review of 544 children over 5.5 years, whose own title calls the protocol high-dose, so it is not a general dexmedetomidine dose. Ahmed 2015 (Int J Pediatr), PMID 25705231.
  5. Note
    Open and tested before the first dose, not findable. Suction connected and running. A self-inflating bag with a mask that fits this face. Oxygen flowing. An oral airway and a supraglottic airway sized to this child, a laryngoscope and tubes, and vascular access you have already flushed.

    A natural-airway sedation can become an airway emergency, so the equipment on this row is the intubation trolley, staged for a child who is not intubated. The person watching the child is the rescuer, and the rescue equipment is theirs to check.

    The guideline requires age- and size-appropriate airway and vascular-access equipment, physiologic monitoring during and after the procedure, and enough trained staff to both do the procedure and watch the patient. Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
  6. Note
    Ask the airway history first, the way you would before an intubation: prior difficult intubation or difficult mask ventilation, prior tracheostomy or subglottic stenosis, a syndrome, a recent extubation failure. That answer decides what is open on the trolley rather than in a drawer.

    Suction connected, turned on, tested, and the catheter within reach of the hand that will need it.

    Oral and nasopharyngeal airways out and to hand, with a size either side of your first choice. A supraglottic airway sized by weight; the LMA Classic bands are in the Airway Adjuncts note on the intubation checklist.

    Carried over from the intubation checklist, which asks the same questions of the same equipment. Difficult bag-mask ventilation was reported in 9.5% of 15,810 PICU intubations across 46 units and was independently associated with adverse events, odds ratio 2.28 (2.03–2.57). Daigle 2020 (Crit Care Med), PMID 32590390.
  7. Note
    Reversal agents for what you are giving, plus epinephrine and atropine drawn or immediately available, dosed to this child's weight in kg.The guideline requires appropriate medications and reversal agents, not sedatives alone. Coté and Wilson 2019, PMID 31138666.
